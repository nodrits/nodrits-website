What a Good Business Website Must Include (Checklist)

October 2026 · 7 min read · By Nodrits Solutions

Not all websites are equal. Some bring in customers every week; others just sit there looking pretty. The difference is rarely the design budget — it's whether the site includes the essentials. Here's the checklist we use for every Nodrits project.

The non-negotiables

  • 1. Clear headline in 5 seconds. A visitor should instantly understand what you do and who it's for. No clever-but-confusing slogans.
  • 2. Mobile-first design. Most visitors arrive on a phone. If your site is hard to use on mobile, it's hard to use — full stop.
  • 3. WhatsApp button. In Pakistan, this is the "call now" button. It should be visible on every page, one tap to chat.
  • 4. Fast loading. Every extra second of load time loses visitors. Optimized images and clean code aren't optional.
  • 5. Contact form that works. Short, simple, and tested — with notifications so you actually see every enquiry.
  • 6. Location & timings. An embedded Google Map plus your address and hours. Don't make customers ask.

The trust builders

  • 7. Real photos. Your shop, your team, your products. Stock photos are fine for decoration, but real photos build trust.
  • 8. Clear pricing or price ranges. "DM for price" frustrates customers. Even a starting price filters serious buyers in.
  • 9. Social proof (when you have it). Reviews, testimonials, client logos — real ones only. Never fake these; customers can tell, and it destroys trust permanently.
  • 10. About page with a human face. People buy from people. A short story and a real photo beat a corporate paragraph.

The growth multipliers

  • 11. Basic SEO. Proper page titles, descriptions and headings so Google understands your business and local customers can find you.
  • 12. A way to capture leads. A form, a chatbot, a newsletter signup — something that turns today's visitor into tomorrow's customer, even if they don't buy now.
A website missing half this list isn't saving money — it's quietly losing customers every single day.
